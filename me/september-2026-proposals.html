The shape of it

Dropping a night made everything cheaper

Moving to Tuesday-to-Saturday took roughly €100–230 a head off every option compared with the five-night version — partly the lost night, partly because Saturday is a far cheaper day to fly home than Sunday. All four now sit between €1,095 and €1,308, comfortably inside the ceiling.

NAP

Vico Equense, Bay of Naples

Naples · Italy · Cheapest

The option where not having a car is an advantage rather than a compromise. Vico Equense sits on the Circumvesuviana railway between Naples and Sorrento: Pompeii is twenty minutes one way, Sorrento fifteen the other, and the Sorrento ferry terminal puts Capri within an easy morning.

At €1,095 a head it comes in €405 under the ceiling — by far the most headroom of the four, and enough to upgrade the rooms or simply eat very well for four days.

Who it suits

A group that wants to do things — an island, a Roman city, three or four towns — and treats the hotel as somewhere to sleep and swim between them.

Main trade-offTwo things: the rooms are a plain 15 m², and the Saturday flight leaves at 10:05, so you're up and out around half seven on the last morning. Four nights here is really three and a half days.

Getting around without a car

Naples → Vico Equense · ~45 min

From arrivals, the Curreri coach runs direct to Sorrento (about €12 a head) and you double back one stop on the train; a private minivan straight to the hotel is simpler with four people and luggage. After that the Circumvesuviana railway does everything — Pompeii, Sorrento, Naples — and ferries leave Sorrento for Capri through the day.

Estimated cost: €180 return for airport transfers, plus about €160 across four days of trains and ferries. Estimate — confirm

The four nights

Arrival through departure
  • Tue 15Down to the bay

    Land 12:20, transfer along the coast, checked in by half two. Afternoon at the pool, evening walk through Vico Equense — a working Italian town rather than a resort.

  • Wed 16Capri

    Train to Sorrento, ferry across. The Blue Grotto if the sea is flat, the chairlift up Monte Solaro if it isn't, and a long lunch in Anacapri before the late boat back.

  • Thu 17Pompeii, then nothing

    Twenty-five minutes on the train, three hours in the ruins before the heat peaks. Back for the pool and an early dinner.

  • Fri 18Sorrento

    The cliff-top town properly: Villa Comunale, the Marina Grande for fish, the ceramic shops, and a last sunset drink looking at Vesuvius.

  • Sat 19Early doors

    Up around half seven, transfer to Naples for the 10:05. Home at 12:45 with the whole weekend still in front of you.

Before you book

  • The 10:05 Saturday flight costs you the last morning. It was the only same-ticket return that day. If a full final day matters more than the money, Marbella and Palma both fly home in the evening.
  • The plainest rooms on this page. 15 m², simple, no frills — you are buying the location and the day trips. There is €405 a head of headroom if you want to spend it on something better.
  • It's Vico Equense, not Sorrento. Fifteen minutes by train from Sorrento proper — quieter and far cheaper, but you're commuting to the famous bits.
  • easyJet fares are cabin-bag only. The €260 baggage line is an estimate at roughly €32 per bag per leg.
  • Transfers are estimates. Curreri coach fares and private minivan rates weren't quotable live.
PMI

Palma, Mallorca

Palma · Balearic Islands · Best Value

The shortest flight of the four at two and a half hours, the second-cheapest fare, and a hotel with two pools a few streets back from the Paseo Marítimo. Santa Catalina market is ten minutes on foot and the cathedral about twenty — which is the whole argument for Palma over a resort.

The Saturday flight doesn't leave until 19:55, so the final day is a real one: pools in the morning, a last lunch, and still home by half ten.

Who it suits

A group that gets restless in a resort and wants a city to walk out into in the evening, but still wants a pool for the afternoon.

Main trade-offThe outbound doesn't land until 18:40, so the first day is really just dinner. And the two rooms are twins — four single beds — which needs a request if you'd rather have doubles.

Getting around without a car

PMI → Palma · 8 km · ~15 min

The shortest airport run of the four — fifteen minutes by taxi, roughly €35 for a van with four people and bags. After that the city does the work: the old town is walkable, the Sóller vintage train leaves from Plaça d'Espanya in the centre, and buses run along the bay to the better beaches at Illetas and Cala Major.

Estimated cost: €70 return by taxi, plus about €80 across four days of buses and local taxis. Estimate — confirm

The four nights

Arrival through departure
  • Tue 15Land and eat

    In at 18:40, taxi fifteen minutes, checked in by half seven. Straight out into Santa Catalina for a first dinner — no ambition beyond that.

  • Wed 16The old town

    La Seu cathedral in the morning light, the Arab baths, Bellver Castle above the city. Lunch at the Mercat de Santa Catalina, afternoon on the roof by the pool.

  • Thu 17Sóller by wooden train

    The 1912 train from Plaça d'Espanya through the Tramuntana orange groves, then the open-sided tram down to Port de Sóller for lunch by the water. No car, all day.

  • Fri 18Beach day

    Bus or taxi fifteen minutes west to Illetas — small coves, clear water, a beach club if you want one. Back for a long tapas crawl through Santa Catalina.

  • Sat 19A full last day

    Pools in the morning, late checkout, bags at reception, a final lunch by the water. Airport at six for the 19:55. Home at 22:30.

Before you book

  • You lose the first day. The 16:15 departure is the only easyJet outbound on the Tuesday; landing at 18:40 means the first evening is dinner and bed. The alternative Transavia pairing runs €1,296 and isn't worth it.
  • Twin beds. Four singles across two rooms. Spanish twins can usually be joined into doubles, but request it rather than assume it.

  • City beaches are ordinary. If lying on sand all day is the point, Marbella or Tenerife will serve you better.
AGP

Marbella, Costa del Sol

Málaga · Spain · Most Relaxing

This is the one that gets the shape of the trip right. You land at 10:30 on Tuesday and don't fly home until 19:10 on Saturday — so four nights buys you four genuinely full days, which none of the others quite manage.

Two Superior rooms with terraces at a hotel scoring 8.8, walking distance to both Marbella's old town and the beach, with free cancellation. Pound for pound it's the most usable week here.

Who it suits

A group that wants beach, town and pool all reachable on foot, and values a long first day and a long last day over a slightly cheaper fare.

Main trade-offThe most expensive flights of the four at €1,081, and the longest transfer of the Spanish options at forty minutes. Breakfast isn't included either.

Getting around without a car

AGP → Marbella · 55 km · ~40 min

Book a private transfer rather than joining the taxi queue — roughly €75 each way for a van, forty minutes down the AP-7. Once there the old town, the beach and the promenade are all on foot, Puerto Banús is a €15–25 taxi, and Ronda runs as a coach day trip with hotel pickup.

Estimated cost: €150 return for private transfers, plus about €100 across four days of local taxis. Estimate — confirm

The four nights

Arrival through departure
  • Tue 15On the sand by lunch

    Land 10:30, transfer down the coast, checked in around noon. The beach for the whole afternoon and a first dinner in the old town.

  • Wed 16Nothing at all

    Pool in the morning, beach after lunch, an evening walk along the promenade. The day the trip justifies itself.

  • Thu 17Ronda

    A coach day trip inland: the Puente Nuevo over the gorge, the old bullring, a bodega lunch. Collected from and returned to the hotel.

  • Fri 18Old town and the marina

    Marbella's Casco Antiguo on foot — orange trees, whitewash, lunch on Calle Ancha — then a taxi to Puerto Banús for the boats and the people-watching.

  • Sat 19A genuinely full last day

    The flight isn't until 19:10. Late checkout, bags with reception, a final long afternoon on the beach before the 16:00 transfer. Home at 22:10.

TFS

Costa Adeje, Tenerife

Tenerife South · Canary Islands · Bougie Pick

The only genuine five-star that fits, and the best-reviewed hotel on this page: 9.2 across 2,294 reviews, with a spa, a serious breakfast and colonial-style gardens above the Costa Adeje seafront.

It's flown by TUI, which matters more than it sounds — the fare includes checked baggage, so the €1,180 headline is the real number rather than the start of one. Against easyJet's €260-ish bag bill, that closes most of the gap to the cheaper options.

Who it suits

A group that wants the hotel to be the holiday, guaranteed September heat, and a twenty-minute transfer from the runway to a sun lounger.

Main trade-offThe longest flight at nearly five hours, an evening arrival, and a return that lands at 00:15 on Sunday morning. You get your Sunday, but you'll spend the first half of it asleep.

Getting around without a car

TFS → Costa Adeje · 20 km · ~20 min

A taxi from Tenerife South runs about €40 for the four of you and takes twenty minutes up the TF-1 — the shortest transfer here. Once there you genuinely don't need anything: the promenade connects the beaches, restaurants and Puerto Colón, and Teide and whale-watching trips both run coaches that collect from Costa Adeje hotels.

Estimated cost: €80 return by taxi, plus about €120 across four days of local taxis. Estimate — confirm

The four nights

Arrival through departure
  • Tue 15Late in, straight to dinner

    Land 18:55, taxi twenty minutes, checked in before eight. A drink in the gardens, dinner at the hotel, early night.

  • Wed 16Duque and back

    Playa del Duque in the morning, pool in the afternoon. Walk the coastal path to La Caleta for fish at sunset.

  • Thu 17Teide

    A coach excursion up to the national park — cable car to 3,555 m if the wind allows, then the caldera floor. Collected from and returned to the hotel.

  • Fri 18Whales, then the spa

    Catamaran from Puerto Colón, ten minutes along the front — resident pilot whales and bottlenose dolphins year-round. Spa and pool for the afternoon.

  • Sat 19Borrowed hours

    Late checkout and the pool until mid-afternoon, taxi at half four for the 18:30. Home at 00:15 on Sunday.

Before you book

  • You land at 00:15 on Sunday. Still home before Monday, but plan how you get back from Schiphol after midnight — and don't schedule anything for Sunday morning.
  • Confirm the room category. Booking quoted €2,311 for two rooms with breakfast and free cancellation, but the property page listed higher-tier Triple Superior rooms at more. The €2,311 is real; pin down exactly which room it buys.
  • Bags are included — but verify the allowance. The TUI fare showed no cabin-bag-only restriction, which normally means hold luggage is in. Confirm the weight limit at booking rather than at the airport.
  • Evening arrival. Landing at 18:55 means the first day is dinner only, same as Palma.
  • Tenerife North was checked and has no nonstop service from Amsterdam, so Tenerife South is the only way in.

Why these dates

Saturday is the cheap day to fly home. Sunday is the expensive one.

Every combination inside your window was priced for four adults, nonstop, both legs after 07:00, on a single ticket. Tuesday-to-Saturday came out cheapest or near-cheapest on three of the four routes — and it puts everyone back in Amsterdam with a clear Sunday.
